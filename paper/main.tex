\title{EL: Trend-to-Product Ranking with Bayesian HIL Calibration}

\author{%
\IEEEauthorblockN{\small Divyesh Sharma, Sankalan Ghosh, Atharva Varshney, Rohan Kumar P, Jivendra Kumar Choudhary, Sourabha Srinivas}
\IEEEauthorblockA{R.V.\ College of Engineering, Bengaluru, India}

\begin{abstract}
We cast trend-driven dropshipping as a constrained daily ranking problem over noisy Indian trend streams. EL is a deployed 70-node n8n workflow that instantiates this problem through deterministic topic scoring, overlap-based deduplication, LLM-mediated contraction, and Bayesian recalibration from Telegram feedback. The front-end score is additive and inspectable; the feedback layer is a closed-form Beta--Bernoulli shrinkage rule with constant-time updates. On the \mbox{2026-04-26} production trace, EL produced 10 curated opportunities and 30 persisted product rows. Under a controlled synthetic-feedback evaluation on this trace, calibration improves mean Spearman correlation from $0.45$ to $0.85$ and reduces mean Brier score from $0.335$ to $0.243$. The contribution is a mathematically explicit operational pipeline rather than an opaque model family.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
ranking, Bayesian calibration, workflow automation, human-in-the-loop, e-commerce.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem}

Let $T_d=\{t_i\}_{i=1}^{n_d}$ denote the day-$d$ topic multiset collected from heterogeneous public sources, and let $P_d$ denote the induced pool of supplier or marketplace products reachable from those topics. The target object is not a trend list but a bounded operator-review set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:objective}
\Pi_d^\star=\arg\max_{\Pi\subseteq P_d}\sum_{p\in\Pi}U(p)
\quad \text{s.t.}\quad |\Pi|\le K,\; A\Pi\le b,
\end{equation}
where $U(p)$ is latent review utility, $K$ is the daily queue budget, and $A\Pi\le b$ encodes hard topic and provider caps. Because $U$ is unobserved, $t\mapsto p$ is many-to-many, and external APIs are volatile, EL adopts a hybrid decomposition into deterministic filtering, tool-augmented contraction, and posterior correction rather than a single learned predictor \cite{n8ndocs,yao2023react,wu2022humanloop}.

\section{Ranking Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

\textbf{Phase 1: deterministic trend scoring.} For topic $t$, let $h(t)$ be the lowercase concatenation of title and related-query hints. EL assigns
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intent}
\begin{split}
s(t)=\mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\bigl(
0.30\,m_{T_1}(t)+0.15\,m_{T_2}(t)\\
+0.10\,m_{T_3}(t)-0.20\,m_N(t)
\bigr),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $m_S(t)=\sum_{k\in S}\mathbf{1}[k\in h(t)]$ counts lexicon hits, $T_1,T_2,T_3$ are transactional, comparative, and product-context lexicons, and $N$ is a negative-intent lexicon. The score is non-parametric, reproducible, and cost-free at inference time. A parallel dictionary map emits one or more coarse product categories from a fixed 13-class taxonomy.

\textbf{Deduplication.} With stopword-filtered token set $w(t)$, EL merges $t_i,t_j$ whenever $|w(t_i)\cap w(t_j)|/\min\{|w(t_i)|,|w(t_j)|\}>0.70$, retaining the representative with richer side context.

\textbf{Opportunity contraction and enrichment.} The top-$30$ topics are passed to a Gemini 2.5 Flash agent with Tavily retrieval and Postgres memory. The agent emits at most $10$ opportunities with raw score $r\in[0,10]$. Each opportunity then fans into a structured CJ branch and a marketplace branch with Tavily search, Browserbase fallback, and Gemini extraction for content-thin pages \cite{browserbasedocs}. Both branches normalize to a common review schema.

\textbf{Constrained queue construction.} Selection is greedy under explicit caps: minimum score, per-topic cap, per-provider cap, and total queue cap. The deployed main workflow contains 70 nodes, of which 60 are functional; a separate 3-node error workflow handles uncaught execution failures.

\section{Feedback and Evidence}
\label{sec:evidence}

Telegram callbacks are interpreted as Bernoulli supervision rather than terminal moderation. For category $c\in\mathcal{C}$ and callback $y\in\{0,1\}$ (\emph{approve}=1, \emph{reject}=0; \emph{skip} ignored), EL maintains $\theta_c\mid\mathcal{D}_c\sim\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_c,\beta_c)$ with update $(\alpha_c,\beta_c)\leftarrow(\alpha_c+y,\beta_c+1-y)$. With posterior mean $\hat{\mu}_c=\alpha_c/(\alpha_c+\beta_c)$ and effective sample size $n_c=(\alpha_c+\beta_c)-2$, the calibrated score is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cal}
\mathrm{cal}(p)=w_c\hat{\mu}_c+(1-w_c)\frac{r(p)}{10},
\qquad
w_c=\frac{n_c}{n_c+n_0},\quad n_0=5.
\end{equation}
Hence cold start yields identity calibration, while repeated feedback shifts ranking mass toward category-specific empirical approval rates. The update is conjugate, exact, and constant-time \cite{bishop2006prml}.

The documented production execution on \mbox{2026-04-26} produced 10 curated opportunities and 30 persisted product rows. Raw opportunity scores ranged from $7.5$ to $8.8$ with mean $8.05$. The observed persisted categories were \emph{Pumps}, \emph{Rings}, and \emph{Blouses \& Shirts}. Because a sufficiently large real callback corpus does not yet exist, the feedback layer was validated under a controlled synthetic model
defined by $\Pr(Y=1\mid r,c)=\sigma(0.8(r-8)+b_c)$ with $b=(-1.5,+1.5,0)$. Across $1000$ bootstrap train/test splits ($15/15$), the raw score $r/10$ achieved mean Spearman $\rho=0.446$ and mean Brier score $0.335$, whereas \cref{eq:cal} achieved $\rho=0.853$ and Brier $0.243$ \cite{guo2017calibration}. The implication is not that EL has solved real-world product selection, but that when reviewer preference contains a category effect orthogonal to the agent score, a conjugate posterior can recover that effect under sparse supervision.

Three caveats delimit the claim: the trace is single-run, the calibration evidence is synthetic, and the enrichment branches remain exposed to exogenous API drift. The next empirical step is to replace this trace with a longitudinal callback corpus and compare the deterministic front-end and Bayesian rear-end against learned baselines on labeled intent data. Until then, EL is best read as a dense systems paper: mathematically explicit, operationally grounded, and deliberately narrow in its claims.
